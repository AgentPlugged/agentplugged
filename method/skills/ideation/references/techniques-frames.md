# Frames: a structure that makes the questions come

- SCAMPER: seven prompts on what exists: swap, merge, adjust, change, repurpose, remove, invert. Use to improve a thing already there.
- Six hats: look in turn at facts, feelings, risks, gains, new ideas and the way of working, one hat at a time. Use to stop one voice from dominating.
- Eight sketches: fold a page into eight panels and fill each with a quick idea, a minute apiece. Use to force quantity before taste.
- How might we: turn the problem into "how might we…?" at several widths, then answer each. Use at the start, to open the field.
- Job to be done: "when…, I want…, so I can…", then ideas per job. Use when you know the need but not the feature.
- Empathy map: what the user voices, believes, does and senses; ideas from each pain and gain. Use when the user is known only from outside.
- Mind map: the question in the middle, branches, branches of branches. Use for a big topic with many sides.
- Starbursting: the six interrogative words aimed at the idea, questions only, no answers yet. Use to find what is not known.
- Storyboard: draw the use of the product in six frames, then ask what each frame could do better. Use to spot weak moments in a flow.
- Cross grid: list users on one side and situations on the other, and fill every cell with an idea. Use to cover cases nobody thought of.
