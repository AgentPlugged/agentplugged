# Narrowing down

Start only once the ideas stop coming, or when the creator asks to choose, rank or get concrete. Say
aloud that the mode changes. Never during a technique that still yields, never inside a batch: an early
verdict silences the next good idea. The stance carries over: in `questions` they decide and you rank
nothing; in `exchange` your view is marked as yours; in `proposal` you may offer a first scoring.

1. **Lay out what exists.** Every live candidate in the file, the strange and forgotten ones as
   well as the latest; say how many.
2. **Pick one tool for this goal and name it**, instead of offering a menu:
   - Group by theme: lots of loose ideas. Cluster, label each cluster, say what links them.
   - Impact and effort: the aim is to act. Plot each idea on both axes; start with big payoff for small
     work, set the rest aside.
   - New, useful, feasible: freshness matters. Score the three from 1 to 10; the totals expose the
     underdogs and the brilliant impossibilities.
   - Forced ranking: only a short podium is wanted. Pit ideas against each other, no ties; give points
     to vote when several people decide.
   - Plus, minus, interesting: one strong idea needs a stress test. List what it has going for it,
     against it, and what is merely curious, then weigh.
   - Must, should, could, not now: a build is being scoped. Put every idea in one of the four bins.
3. **Take it through** and record it under `## Convergence`: one line `Move: <name>` per tool, then the
   outcome. A second tool may follow the first (cluster, then score the clusters); a third is refused.
4. **Stop** when a short list or one direction stands out. The creator says which ideas stay, in their
   words; you carry the list to `## Kept` when closing (`references/close.md`).
