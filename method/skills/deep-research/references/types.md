# Types of research

Types go in the page's `research.types`. The question decides; several may apply. Each sub-question
goes to one sub-agent (as many as the plan allows). Primary sources first: `references/page-format.md`.
Each type has a window (a source published longer ago does not back a claim) and claims to
cross-check (the ones a conclusion rests on: mark them `key`).

- `market`: size, growth, segments, prices, what buyers pay for. Primary: filings, official
  statistics, regulators, the sellers' own price pages. Secondary: analyst notes, the press.
  Sub-questions: size and growth, segments, prices, trends. Tips: a listed company's annual report
  often sizes its field; a price comes from the seller's page, not from a comparison article;
  harsh reviews show the pains. Window: 18 months, a price 3. Cross-check: sizes, growth, a rival's traction.
- `domain`: the vocabulary, rules, actors and regulations of a field. Primary: the text of the law
  or standard, the regulator's page, the official specification. Secondary: guides, summaries.
  Sub-questions: terms, rules, actors, obligations. Window: 3 years, a rule in force 1.
  Cross-check: every legal obligation.
- `technical`: how a technology works, its limits, versions, advisories, costs. Primary: official
  documentation, release notes, the source repository, RFCs, advisories. Secondary: blogs, forums.
  Always write the version or the date a fact holds for. Window: 1 year, a version or a price 3
  months. Cross-check: limits, compatibility, security, prices.
- `competitive`: who else answers the problem, what they offer, at what price, where they fall
  short. Primary: their own site, price page, documentation, changelog, public repository.
  Secondary: reviews, comparisons. One sub-question per competitor. Window: 6 months, a price 3.
  Cross-check: prices, traction, whatever decides between two rivals.
- `user_voice`: what users say in public (forums, reviews, issue trackers). The post itself is
  primary, but it is an opinion, not a fact: count what you read ("9 of the 30 reviews read"), never
  write "users want". Quote without the author's name, handle, e-mail or profile link. A summary
  article is secondary. Window: 2 years; a complaint repeated over the years weighs more.
- `literature`: papers and studies. Primary: the paper itself (publisher, DOI, preprint); say which
  of the three. Secondary: summaries and news. Put the authors and the year in the claim. No
  window: a paper does not age; a newer one that contradicts it is a finding.
