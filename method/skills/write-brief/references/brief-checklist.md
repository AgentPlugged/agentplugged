# Brief checklist

Run every check before showing the brief to the creator. Fix what fails; when a fix needs the
creator, ask them, one question at a time.

## Header

- The header is the first thing in the file and holds `id: brief`, `kind: brief`, `owner: iris`
  and `links`.
- `links` names only documents that exist (the research notes the brief uses).
- No `status`, `state`, `cost`, `spent`, `budget_spent` or `progress` key.
- `npx agentplugged docs check` reports no problem.

## Substance

- The problem statement is one sentence, in the creator's words, and names who has the problem.
- The problem comes before the solution; the brief would still make sense if the proposed
  answer changed.
- Each group of users has a job to get done and a current workaround.
- The value is an outcome the users would notice, not a list of features.
- Every figure, quote and market claim names its source, or carries `[assumption]`.
- The riskiest assumptions each have a test, and the riskiest one is first.
- The scope says what is out as clearly as what is in, and lists what is still open.
- The success signals can be observed by someone outside the team.
- The open questions are real unknowns, not tasks.

## Form

- One to two pages; detail that does not fit lives in a research note.
- No requirement identifier is defined (`EF`, `ENF`, `PU`, `MS` belong to the PRD).
- No section is padded: a section that does not apply says so in one line.
- No `<…>` placeholder is left from the template.
- No guidance comment and no "Generated by agentplugged" line is left in the brief.
- Each research note follows `assets/research-note-template.md`: `id` of the form
  `research-<topic>`, `kind: note`, `owner: iris`, sources named.
- No `[open: G-…]` mark remains for a decision the creator already answered.
- The brief is written in the creator's language and reads without this skill at hand.
