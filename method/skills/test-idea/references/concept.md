# The kind of concept

Name it early, from what they say, and write it in the header (`concept:`): it decides who the
"reader" is and what the questions weigh. Ask only when it is unclear.

| `concept:` | The reader is | The questions weigh | The release ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| `commercial` | a buyer | price, alternatives, how the first customers come, whether the company lasts | how to start |
| `internal` | colleagues | effort to adopt, who sponsors it, upkeep, why leave the current way | how to roll it out |
| `open-source` | users and contributors | who maintains it, licence, why trust a project that may stall | how to take part |
| `community` | members | who takes part, who steers, how it is paid for over time | how to join |

A tool built for oneself is `internal`. Name the reader in the creator's words, never "everyone".

Whatever the type, begin with the person, not the product:

- They open with what they want to build: ask who has the trouble, at what moment, and what they do
  about it today.
- They open with a technique (a model, a protocol): ask what the person would lose without it; if
  nobody would notice, the idea is not there yet.
- Two or three exchanges later there is still no reader and no felt trouble: stop pushing and go back
  to the forge, or to a round of ideas (skill `ideation`, Lou).

When they are stuck, offer two or three readings to react to, not the same question again.
