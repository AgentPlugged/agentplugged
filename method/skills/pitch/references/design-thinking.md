# Design thinking, the course

Keep the user at the centre; judge late; make things tangible fast; a failed test is information.
Start from what the documents say of the user (PRD `PU-n`, personas of `ux.md`, research pages). When
no research exists the creator speaks for the users: say so and mark it `[to verify]`.

0. **Challenge.** Ask: the problem or opportunity, who is concerned, the constraints (budget,
   technology), what success looks like, what research exists. Write one challenge sentence: it opens the
   **Definition** section.
1. **Empathy.** Pick exercises (`design-methods.md`). Gather what users say, think, do, feel; the pains,
   the surprises, the patterns. Ask who the creator can really watch or interview.
2. **Definition.** "<user> needs <need> because <insight>", then 3 to 5 "How might we" questions;
   name the assumptions behind them and cluster the insights.
3. **Ideas.** Diverge first: 15 to 30 ideas, no judging, build on each other, wild and practical. Then
   cluster, and the creator keeps two or three against the need and the constraints.
4. **Prototype.** The least that tests the riskiest assumption: what to learn, what the user must be able
   to do, what can be faked. Say what it leaves out.
5. **Test.** Five to seven people, one task each; watch what they do more than what they say. Capture:
   worked, struggled, surprised, questions, changes. Say which assumptions held or fell. Results are the
   creator's words, dated, never invented.
6. **Next round.** Refine, reframe, return to empathy, or pilot with real users. Name the priority action,
   who is involved, and how success will be measured (one metric, one date).

7. **Implementation.** `design-implementation.md`: test plan, feedback to collect, then each learning becomes a
   hypothesis followed or a proposed story, one gesture each.

Choose by need: unclear problem, steps 0 to 2; known problem, 3 to 5; a built idea, 5 to 7. A step not run
stays in the file with one line saying why.
