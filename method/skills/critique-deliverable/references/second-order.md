# Second-order effects

A good decision is judged by what it causes next, and next again.

1. Pick the decisions and requirements that change behaviour (a limit, a default, a price, a
   rule, an automation).
2. For each, write the first consequence, then what that causes in turn, in the users, the
   team, the data, the costs, a year on. Include who finds a way around it.
3. Keep the chains that end somewhere the document did not want, or that cancel its goal.

Each is an objection: the end of the chain is the `claim`, the decision that starts it is the
`passage`, and the early sign that it is happening is the `proof`.
