# The methods

One line each. Every kept item is an objection: `claim` (what you found), `passage` (where the
document allows it), `proof` (the cheapest check). Keep what the document does not answer; worst first.

- `pre_mortem`: a year on it failed; write the 3 to 5 likeliest causes, keep those a passage allowed.
- `red_team`: be the adversary (misuse, attack, load, a dependency down); keep the cheap breaks nothing rules out.
- `socratic`: ask of each premise why we believe it and what follows if false; keep the unevidenced.
- `first_principles`: rebuild the simplest answer from the bare need; keep habit-weight and gaps.
- `devils_advocate`: argue the opposite of each main choice, fairly; keep what the document does not answer.
- `inversion`: list the sure ways to fail; keep what the document does or allows that leans that way.
- `divergence`: two teams build two parts, each obeying every decision of the document exactly as written; keep where the parts still do not fit, with the `AD-n` that should have closed it.
- `assumption_audit`: extract stated and silent assumptions, rank by odds and cost; keep the top few.
- `steelman`: argue the rejected option at its best; keep the choices that lose to it.
- `failure_modes`: per part, how it fails and what the user sees; keep the undetected or unrecovered.
- `second_order`: follow each rule or default to its consequences and workarounds; keep bad endings.
- `domain_expert`: read as a veteran of the field; keep the naive, impossible, illegal or already solved.
- `extreme_user`: walk edge users (expert, newest, offline, abusive) through it; keep gaps many meet.
- `compare_existing`: set it beside what exists (code, documents, products); keep contradictions, duplicates, breakages.
- `reframe`: restate the problem wider, narrower and from the user's side; keep framings where a simpler answer wins.
- `subtraction`: ask what is lost if each part goes; keep what costs to build or read and no journey misses.
