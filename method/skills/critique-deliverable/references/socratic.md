# Socratic

Do not attack; ask until a premise shows. For every important statement of the document, ask:
what do we mean exactly, what makes us believe it, what follows if it is false, what would we
see if it were.

1. List the premises the document stands on (about the user, the market, the problem, the
   constraints), stated or not.
2. For each, ask the four questions and answer them from the document alone. Where the document
   has no answer, that is the finding.
3. Keep the premises without evidence that the document builds on.

An objection is a premise taken for granted: the premise is the `claim`, the passage that relies
on it is the `passage`, and the cheapest way to check it (an interview, a number, a prototype)
is the `proof`.
