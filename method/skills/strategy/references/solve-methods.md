# Methods by step

Offer two or three that fit the problem and the creator's taste (systematic or playful); say in a line
when each works best and what to ask. Do not run them all.

**Problem and causes (diagnosis)**
- Statement sharpening: turn a complaint into one precise sentence; who, what, where, the gap, the stake.
- Is / is not: where, when, who and what it is and is not; the differences point at the cause.
- Five whys: a chain of "why" on one symptom; good for a single line of cause, stop at what can be acted on.
- Cause map (fishbone): causes by family (people, process, tools, inputs, environment, measures); good for many factors.
- Systems view: parts, links, feedback loops, delays, leverage points; good when fixing one thing breaks another.

**Forces and constraints (analysis)**
- Force field: forces for and against the change, rated; remove an opposing force before adding a push.
- Vital few (80/20): count the causes by frequency or impact and take the few that carry most of it.
- Gap analysis: current against wanted state, one dimension at a time, with the size of each gap.
- Bottleneck search: find the one constraint that limits the whole; improve there, nowhere else first.
- Failure modes: for each step of a fix, how it fails, how likely, how bad, how to prevent or detect it.

**Options (synthesis and creative)**
- Contradiction matrix (TRIZ): name what improves and what worsens, then borrow from the inventive
  principles that resolved the same contradiction elsewhere (split, merge, invert, do it in advance...).
- Lateral thinking: a provocation or a random entry to leave the usual path, then find the sense in it.
- Morphological grid: the parameters of a solution, the choices for each, combine the unusual ones.
- Nature analogy: how living systems solve the same function; what principle transfers.
- Analogy chain (synectics): make the strange familiar and the familiar strange through metaphors.
- Assumption busting: list what is taken for granted, flip each one, see what becomes possible.
- Random word: a random word forced into relation with the problem; keep the sparks.
- Reverse brainstorm: how to make the problem happen or get worse, then turn each answer around.
- Six hats: facts, feelings, benefits, risks, creativity, process, one at a time, on the same option.
- Seven lenses on what exists: swap, merge, adapt, change, repurpose, remove, reverse.

**Choice (evaluation)**
- Weighted matrix: options against weighted criteria, scored, the total read with suspicion.
- Cost and benefit: what it costs and brings, in the same unit, payback; every cost with its basis.
- Risk grid: likelihood against impact for each option; deal with the top right first.
- Trial protocol: the smallest experiment, its success criterion, the data, the decision it feeds.
- Feasibility: technical, operational, financial and organisational, one verdict each.

**Plan and checks (implementation)**
- Plan, do, check, adjust: a loop with a review after each pass.
- Sequence and dependencies: tasks, order, what blocks what, milestones that prove progress.
- People map: who is affected, how much they can help or block, what each needs to hear.
- Change handling: what changes for whom, the resistance to expect, how to tell it.
- Indicator board: few measures, a target and a reading rhythm each, a threshold that triggers a decision.
