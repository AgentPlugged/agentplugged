# Innovation strategy, the course

Tell the truth about the market before looking for a breakthrough; a flattering picture sinks a strategy.
Push for evidence over hope, a lasting advantage over a clever feature, a bold idea over a safe one that
changes nothing, and keep the plan one a solo creator can carry. The creator is not asked to know the
frameworks: you choose them (`innovate-frameworks.md`, two to four per step), say in a line what each
reveals, and ask the questions. The sections of the file are the steps; a step not run keeps its
section with one line saying why.

1. **Context.** The business or product, what drives the exploration (pressure, an opening, a plateau),
   the current model in brief, the limits (money, time, rules, skills), and what a breakthrough would look
   like. Start from the brief, the positioning and the story if they exist.
2. **Market.** Segments and how they move; the real competitors, the unexpected ones and the substitutes;
   what is changing; who is underserved and who overserved. Reuse the research pages (`docs research`);
   search only what is missing or old, and every size or share carries its source or `[to verify]`.
3. **Model.** Who is really served and what job they hire the product for; how value is made, delivered
   and captured today; the honest advantage; where the model is vulnerable; the assumptions that may be
   false. Revenue and costs are only named here; their figures go to the economics.
4. **Openings.** Where an entrant could win: non-users to serve, jobs left undone, a good-enough version
   for a new segment, a technology that just made something cheap, a space where competition is
   irrelevant. Name the disruption vectors and the white space.
5. **Options.** Five to ten concrete moves, across the model (how value is made and captured), the value
   chain (what to do, what to buy), partnerships and technology. Mix the near and the far.
6. **Direction.** Merge them into three different strategic options. For each: the direction, what it
   changes in the model, the position against competitors, what it needs, the main risks and
   dependencies, what to expect. Judge them on fit with the creator's strengths, timing, defensibility,
   feasibility and risk against reward. Then recommend one or a combination: why, what gives
   confidence, what worries, the hypotheses to prove first, what would make you change course. The creator
   decides.
7. **Economics.** The cost of the product, the price, the revenue, the break-even point: `costs.md`.
8. **Roadmap.** Three phases, no dates: first the moves that prove the riskiest hypothesis and give
   momentum; then the foundations (capabilities, the first market, steady growth); then scale and
   efficiency. For each: the initiatives, what they need, the measures of success, and the decision gate
   that opens the next.
9. **Risks.** Early signals (adoption, engagement, efficiency) and results (revenue, share, margin); the
   go and no-go gates; what could kill the strategy, the assumptions that may be wrong, how competitors
   may answer, and the backup plan.

Choose by need: no model yet, steps 1 to 3 then 7; a plateau, steps 3 to 6; a decision on price, steps 3
and 7. Then the gestures (`gestures.md`).
