# Frameworks by step

Choose two to four for the step and the stage of the product (an idea, a first customer, a plateau); one
line to the creator on what each reveals. A solo creator has little data: say so, and prefer the light
ones that need only a few conversations.

**Market (step 2)**
- Market size in three rings (total, reachable, obtainable): a realistic first target, not a dream.
- Five forces: suppliers, buyers, rivals, newcomers, substitutes; where the power sits.
- Outside forces (political, economic, social, technical, legal, environmental): which ones matter here.
- Timing: what must be true first, what is changing now, whether customers and the technology are ready.
- Positioning map: two axes that matter to buyers, where each competitor sits, the empty corner.

**Model (step 3)**
- Business model canvas: the nine blocks (segments, value, channels, relationships, revenue, resources,
  activities, partners, costs); mark the weak ones.
- Value fit: the customer's jobs, frictions and benefits against what the product relieves and creates.
- Model patterns: subscription, free then paid, marketplace, razor and blade, licence, usage, bundle; which
  fits the cost and the buyer.
- Revenue ideas: who could pay, for what, how (per seat, per use, per result, one time); what customers
  would accept.
- Cost redesign: the biggest costs, what to remove, automate or buy; the price point that unlocks.

**Openings (step 4)**
- Disruption from below: a simpler, cheaper offer for those the incumbents overlook.
- Jobs to be done: the progress customers seek and the alternatives they hire today.
- Blue ocean: what to eliminate, reduce, raise and create to leave the crowded market.
- Crossing the gap: from the first fans to the mainstream through one narrow starting market.
- Platform: two or more sides that attract each other; what makes the network worth joining.

**Options (step 5)**
- Three horizons: protect the core, build the next, seed the later; balance the three.
- Build, measure, learn: the riskiest assumption, the smallest test, the measure, the pivot.
- Ambition grid: core, adjacent and transformational moves, with the risk of each.
- Strategic intent: a bold aim that stretches the means; what would change the field.
- Scenarios: the key uncertainties, a few futures, a strategy that holds in several.
- Value chain: the whole chain, where value is made and captured, the activities to own.
- Unbundling: what is sold together that could be sold apart, where the value really sits.
- Ecosystem design: sides, exchanges, how to attract each, what is shared.
- Make or buy: the real strength, what gives the advantage, what to own and what to rent.
- Partnerships: who has the missing strength, what each side gives, how the value is split.

**Technology (steps 3 to 5, when the product rests on one)**
- Adoption curve: who adopts when, and what each group needs to hear.
- S-curve: where the technology stands, the next curve, when to jump.
- Technology roadmap: the capabilities needed, their order, their dependencies.
- Open innovation: what to take from outside, what to give, with whom.
- Digital shift: what the digital tools change in the model, the operations and the customer's life.
